About Me

My story so far

I'm a Junior at Michigan State University, studying computer science with a concentration in cybersecurity and a minor in information technology. The concentration teaches me how attackers think. The IT minor keeps me honest about how systems get deployed and maintained in the real world.

Before school got serious about security, I spent time as a website development intern, where I learned that shipping something people actually use is a different skill from getting it to work on my laptop.

I also work as a waiter, and I'll defend this as legitimate engineering experience. A dining room during a Friday rush is a distributed system under load: limited resources, unpredictable inputs, and users who will tell you immediately when something is broken.

How I think about security

Security that people work around isn't security. It's paperwork.

I believe the best defenses are the ones users barely notice. That means clear error messages, sensible defaults, and audit trails that answer questions after something goes wrong. I want a career where I can make systems safer and easier to use, and I don't see those as competing goals.

Projects

A few things I've built or am building, and what each one taught me.

Secure Document Service (Dockerized Flask Microservices)

Course project, CSE 380 · Spring 2026

A document management system split into four independent Flask services, all orchestrated with Docker.

What it does

  • Authenticates users with JSON Web Tokens (JWT)
  • Stores and manages documents
  • Searches across stored documents
  • Records an audit log of who did what

What I learned

  • Passed all 15 of the project's test cases.
  • Splitting a system into services makes trust boundaries explicit: every request between services is a decision about who to believe.
  • Audit logging is much easier to design in from the start than to bolt on later.

Manoo

Team project · React · In progress

Manoo is a neighbor-to-neighbor rental app for Iași, Romania. The idea is simple: people own things they rarely use, and their neighbors need those things occasionally. Manoo connects them.

Highlights

  • Built with a small team using React
  • Includes an earnings calculator that shows owners what their gear could make
  • Includes daily and weekly challenges that keep people coming back

What I learned

Real user feedback is humbling and useful. Testers loved the challenges, found the onboarding too thin, and told us the annual income figure in the calculator confused casual users. The redesign is now driven by what people actually said, not what we assumed.
